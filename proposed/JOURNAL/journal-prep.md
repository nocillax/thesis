Manuscript Preparation

Manuscript length should be 8 to 19 pages, and it should be written in English. MECS journals can exceptionally accept shorter or longer manuscripts, provided that the scientific content is of high value. All submitted manuscripts must include the following items:

-Title

-List of authors, their affiliations, ORCiD and email addresses

-Abstract

-Keywords

-Introduction

-Main body

-Results and discussion

-Conclusions

-Acknowledgments (optional)

-References

1. Additional information regarding the article's structure
   The entire manuscript must be written in English and range in length from eight to nineteen pages,
   including figures and tables. The MECS journals may accept shorter or extended manuscripts on rare
   occasions, provided the scientific content is of high quality. Each submitted manuscript must include:
   Title of article - The article's title should be precise, succinct, abbreviation-free, and precisely reflective
   of its content. According to the capitalization rule, the initial letter of each word in a title should be
   capitalized, while all other sections and words, including prepositions, articles, and conjunctions, should
   be lowercased.
   Authors, affiliations, email addresses, and ORCiD - All authors' complete names and affiliations
   should be provided. Include department, university or organization, city, and country in affiliations. One
   of the authors must be designated as the corresponding author and must include their email address
   and ORCID. At least one of the authors must be from a distinct country in order to maintain the
   advancement and international cooperation of the research.
   Abstract - It should be between 1300 and 2000 characters (including spaces) and convey the article's
   key points. It does not contain abbreviations whose meanings are dependent on their context. In the
   abstract, references, figures, and tables should not be included.
   Keywords - Three to five keywords or phrases are required and must be separated by commas. It does
   not contain abbreviations whose meanings are dependent on their context.
   Introduction - The introduction section of your manuscript should provide context. When writing the
   introduction, please keep in mind that not all of your readers will be experts in your field of study.
   Problem statement and Literature review - Problem statement should describe your problem and its
   significance in words, briefly introduces some background to your problem, and gives your reader some
   context before you describe your problem. Literature review should describe previous work related to
   your problem, tell a coherent story of what the authors of these works did, and how they are related to
   your project.
   Main body - the main body part should include the main proposed ideas, results, and discussions.
   Conclusions - A conclusion is where you summarize and generalize the paper's findings, discuss
   ambiguous data, and suggest additional research. An effective conclusion should bring a paper to a
   close and leave the reader with the sensation that the concepts have been thoroughly explained.
   Appendices - Additional information, such as tables or mathematical calculations or derivations, can
   be included and should be clearly referred to, from the main text, as belonging to the appendix.
   Acknowledgments - As the author, you have the option to include or omit acknowledgments. Typically,
   the acknowledgments section includes the names of individuals who contributed to the work but do not
   meet the criteria for authorship. This section of your manuscript may also contain information about
   potential funding sources.
   Conflict of Interest - There may be a conflict of interest if you, your employer, or your sponsor have
   financial, commercial, legal, or professional ties with other organizations or their employees that could
   influence your research. The essence of a conflict of interest may be financial or non-financial. In order
   to maintain transparency, all relationships that could be perceived as a conflict of interest must also be
   disclosed.
   References – The MECS journals utilize the numbered citation method for formatting references, with
   sequential numbering in the text and respective ordering in the reference list at the conclusion of the
   paper.
   The list should include at least 13 references and be arranged in the same order as they appear in the
   text. The reference list must include at least fifty percent foreign sources. There can be only one
   reference per reference number in the list. Each reference number should be encapsulated in square
   brackets in the text. Citations of references can be presented as "in [6] ..." or "in reference [6] ..."
   Likewise, it is not required to mention the authors of a reference unless it is pertinent to the text.
   Commas should separate multiple citations contained within a single set of brackets. Where there are
   three or more sequential citations, they should be given as a range [5, 8–11, 13].
   Authors must regulate the self-citation rate, and each article may only cite 1-2 of their own published
   works.
   In order to regulate the journal's self-citation rate, each article may only reference one previous
   publication.
   Before submitting the manuscript to the editor, it is necessary to verify the functionality of all URL
   sources.
2. Requirements for titles and subtitles
   Titles and subtitles should be separated from the text by an empty line both above and below. If the text
   is to be divided into chapters and subchapters, multilevel numeration should be used as shown below:
   Chapters - 1, 2, ...,
   Subchapters - 1.1, 1.2, ..., 2.1, 2.2, ...,
   Subchapter sections -1.1.1, 1.1.2, ..., 1.2.1, 1.2.2, ...,
   If there are fourth level titles, please number the titles with Roman numerals “i, ii, iii…”; If there are
   titles under the fourth level title, please number the titles with English letter “a, b, c…”.
3. Requirements for formatting figures
   In general, inserting the images into the document is sufficient; however, for security reasons, it is
   preferable to provide the original sources of the figures in a standard format such as: dwg, vsd, cdr, eps,
   tif, bmp, or jpg (i.e., electronic version). Please do not insert the figure directly into the text but use the
   "insert text-box" option, and then insert the figure inside of the text box. In this way, it is easier later to
   move or change the dimensions of the figures.
   A caption should be given to each figure and be placed immediately beneath its figure. The captions
   for the figures should not be included in the figures and should be editable.
   The figures should be numbered consecutively throughout the manuscript using the single-number
   system (for example: as: Fig. 1, Fig. 2, Fig. 3…). Figures must be mentioned in the text in consecutive
   order, all figures must have a brief title accompanied with a short description that can be able to be
   understood without reference to the main text.
   All images must be submitted with a resolution of at least 400 dpi. Scanned figures are not acceptable
   but should be uploaded in the format they were created.
   The inscriptions on the figures should be clear and readable, the lines of the figure should not be blurred.
   There should be no noise in the figure.
   The figures cannot be arbitrarily enlarged or shrunk, as this would cause the figure to be deformed and
   unsightly.
   Try to use drawing tools to draw figures, such as Microsoft Office Visio, etc.
   The editorial board reserves the right to reject a manuscript if the authors refuse to provide the original
   figure files in an effort to prevent data falsification.
4. Requirements for the design of tables
   Table captions should be placed above the table, left-aligned, font size 8.
   Table title should look like this: Table 1. Table title.
   Tables must be editable without image format.
   Tables should be numbered in a consistent style, e.g., Table 1, Table 2, Table 3...
   Tables must be mentioned in the text in consecutive order, all tables must have a brief title accompanied
   with a short description that can be able to be understood without reference to the main text.
5. Requirements for formatting of the equations
   It is recommended to use standard equation editor existing in Microsoft Word or the Math Type editor.
   The equation editor is defined as follows: font Times New Roman italic, matrix bold, for letters font 10,
   for index 8, for symbol 12.
   Equations should be numbered, and numbering alignment to the right.
   Only relevant equations should be included in the main text, anything else can be added as an appendix.
   How to write an equation or formula: https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/write-an-equation-orformula-1d01cabc-ceb1-458d-bc70-7f9737722702
6. Word processing formats
   Please ensure that your article is in ".doc" or ".docx" format prior to submission. In order to typeset the
   articles in MECS style, these two article formats are acceptable for the MECS journals.
